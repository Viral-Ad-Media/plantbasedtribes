Thinking about eating more plants? Whether you want to go fully vegan or simply cut back on meat, this guide covers the basics so you can start with confidence.

## What is a plant-based diet?

A plant-based diet focuses on foods that come from plants: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, beans, lentils, nuts and seeds. Some people use the term to mean fully vegan, with no animal products at all. Others use it more loosely to describe a diet that is *mostly* plants. Both are valid. The best approach is the one you can stick to.

## Why people choose it

- **Health:** Diets rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains and legumes are linked with a lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers.
- **Environment:** Plant foods generally need less land and water and produce fewer greenhouse gas emissions than animal foods.
- **Animals:** Many people choose to avoid animal products for ethical reasons.
- **Budget:** Beans, lentils, grains and seasonal vegetables are some of the cheapest foods you can buy.

## What to eat

Build your plate around these food groups:

- **Vegetables and fruit:** Aim for lots of colour. Fresh, frozen and tinned all count.
- **Whole grains:** Oats, brown rice, quinoa, wholewheat pasta, barley and wholegrain bread.
- **Legumes:** Beans, chickpeas, lentils, peas, tofu, tempeh and edamame.
- **Nuts and seeds:** Almonds, walnuts, peanuts, chia, flax, hemp and pumpkin seeds.
- **Healthy fats:** Olive oil, avocado, nuts and seeds.

A simple formula for a balanced plate is half vegetables, a quarter whole grains and a quarter protein-rich legumes or tofu.

## Where do you get your protein?

It's the question every plant-based eater hears. The good news is that most people eating a varied plant-based diet with enough calories get plenty of protein. Beans, lentils, tofu, tempeh, seitan, edamame, nuts, seeds and whole grains all provide it. Read more in our guide to [plant-based protein sources](/plant-based-protein-sources).

## Nutrients to pay attention to

- **Vitamin B12:** This isn't reliably found in plant foods. Anyone eating a fully vegan diet should take a supplement or eat B12-fortified foods.
- **Vitamin D:** Many people, vegan or not, need a supplement in the darker months.
- **Iron:** Found in lentils, beans, tofu, leafy greens and fortified cereals. Eating vitamin C-rich foods at the same meal helps your body absorb it.
- **Calcium:** Choose fortified plant milks and yoghurts, calcium-set tofu, kale and almonds.
- **Omega-3 fats:** Ground flaxseed, chia seeds, hemp seeds and walnuts provide ALA. Some people choose an algae-based supplement.
- **Iodine:** Found in iodised salt and some fortified plant milks.

If you have a health condition, are pregnant or are planning a big dietary change, it's a good idea to talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian.

## Easy swaps to start with

- Swap cow's milk for oat, soya or almond milk.
- Use lentils or beans in place of mince in bolognese, chilli and tacos.
- Try tofu scramble instead of scrambled eggs.
- Choose hummus or avocado instead of butter and cheese on toast.
- Make soups, stews and curries bean- or lentil-based.

## Common beginner mistakes

1. **Not eating enough.** Plant foods are often less calorie-dense, so you may need larger portions.
2. **Relying on processed substitutes.** Vegan burgers and cheeses are fine occasionally, but whole foods should make up most of your diet.
3. **Forgetting B12.** Make supplementing a habit from day one.
4. **Trying to change everything overnight.** Start with a few meals a week and build from there.

## Getting started

Pick three recipes to try this week, stock your pantry with a few staples (tinned beans, lentils, grains, spices and stock) and be kind to yourself. Every plant-based meal counts. Our [7-day meal plan for beginners](/plant-based-diet-meal-plan-for-beginners) is a great next step.
